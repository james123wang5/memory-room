Rebuilding my robot dojo in Blender with AI. I gave Claude a LiDAR scan, 188 reference photos, instructions and feedback. It built the scene using Blender scripts and Tripo\-generated props. The room shell and precise structures were scripted. Fourteen props were generated from cut\-outs of my own photos, then inspected, repaired and fitted back to the real room. The video shows the process: reconstruction, object checks, furnishing, materials and lighting — followed by photo comparisons and day and night walkthroughs.

A single GoPro clip can now be rebuilt into a walkable 4D scene. IronSight takes ordinary 2D video and reconstructs the whole space around it. Every person, every target, every hit and miss gets tracked and placed in 3D over time. You can scrub it like a timeline and orbit it from a God's eye view. You can even see people through walls. For years a camera just recorded pixels. IronSight reads that same footage as evidence of a world with depth, motion, and objects that persist even when the frame looks away. That turns a camera into a world model. The open\-source release drops in July. That's the part that matters. It stops being a lab demo the moment anyone can point it at their own footage. Where does that leave every clip already sitting on your phone?

Another project I built over Christmas. Fabricator It’s a dev environment for designing and sharing parametric 3d printed objects using TypeScript. Because everything is code, you can plug it straight into your favourite coding assistant and have AI generate printable designs. You can even give it a photo or video and say “make me this”. In practice that part is not quite there yet. Getting something genuinely usable still takes a fair bit of human coaching. But it seems to be on the same trajectory as AI coding tools were around 2023… and we all know that has gone. A recent development with Google Gemini is that it can now spit out STL files directly, so possibly my project is obsolete before the paint’s even dry. Building it made one thing clear: designing physical objects is about to get a lot weirder. Screenshot is of a parametric International Space Station AI generated from photos. It highlights the current limitations but it's still an order of magnitude improvement from 12 months ago. Link to code and demo in comments.
